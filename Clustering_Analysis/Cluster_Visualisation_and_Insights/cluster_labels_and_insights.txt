Cluster labels and insights (K-Means, k=5)

Dataset summary
This analysis uses 7,043 customers with K-Means clustering (k=5).
The overall churn rate in the dataset is 26.54%. Cluster churn rates reveal one clearly high-risk segment (Cluster 4) and four segments that are close to the overall average.

Cluster 0 — Long-Term Contract Loyalists (n=1,299)
All customers in this cluster are on Two-year contracts. The churn rate is 23.17%, which is below the overall churn rate (26.54%). PhoneService is 100% “Yes”.
InternetService is mostly DSL (74.29% DSL, 25.71% Fiber optic). MultipleLines is balanced (51.50% Yes, 48.50% No).
The strongest distinguishing feature is contract length. Two-year commitment likely reduces churn because customers face lower switching intention or higher switching cost.
Service mix is relatively standard and does not appear to drive churn strongly.
This segment is stable and can be treated as a retain and grow group.
Recommended actions include renewal planning before contract end, loyalty rewards, and gentle cross-sell/up-sell without heavy discounting.
A practical trigger is to flag customers approaching the final months of a two-year term for proactive retention contact.

Cluster 1 — DSL Month-to-Month (Single-Line) Users (n=2,003)
InternetService is 100% DSL. Contract type is mainly Month-to-month (66.30%) and One-year (33.70%), with no Two-year contracts.
The churn rate is 24.51%. PhoneService is 100% “Yes”. MultipleLines is mostly “No” (80.18% No, 19.82% Yes).
This cluster seems to represent traditional DSL users with simpler setups (single line).
Although churn is not extreme, the high share of month-to-month contracts indicates higher flexibility to leave compared with long-term contract customers.
The key opportunity is to reduce churn risk by improving commitment and perceived value, so client can target offers to convert month-to-month customers into 12-month or 24-month contracts, plus bundle messaging that fits single-line households.
Monitoring should focus on month-to-month users who show early dissatisfaction signals.

Cluster 2 — Fiber Month-to-Month (Multi-Line) Users (n=2,021)
InternetService is 100% Fiber optic. Contract type is heavily Month-to-month (80.36%) with One-year at 19.64% and no Two-year.
The churn rate is 23.50% (below overall). PhoneService is 100% “Yes”. MultipleLines leans “Yes” (59.43% Yes, 40.57% No).
This segment is clearly separated by Fiber service. Despite being mostly month-to-month, the churn rate is still below average.
This suggests that Fiber users may perceive higher value or better performance, or the bundle structure increases stickiness.
Business insight and action: This group should be maintained through service quality consistency and transparent pricing.
Therefore, we can focus on value guarantee plans for month-to-month customers to reduce surprise-bill churn.

Cluster 3 — No Phone Service / Lower Monthly Charge Segment (n=682)
PhoneService is 100% “No”, making this cluster structurally different from others. The churn rate is 24.93% (near overall).
Average monthly charges are lower (about 42.02). Contract  is more balanced compared with other clusters: 52.93% month-to-month, 23.02% one-year, and 24.05% two-year.
InternetService is mixed (55.87% DSL, 44.13% Fiber optic). MultipleLines values still exist in the dataset, but the defining feature remains “no phone service”.
The most likely interpretation is internet-only customers. Their spending level is lower, and churn is close to the average.
This segment may churn due to price/value comparison, moving house, or switching to competitors’ internet-only deals.
The main opportunity is migration into bundles that increase retention such as adding phone service.

Cluster 4 — High-Risk Seniors with High Monthly Charges (n=1,038)
SeniorCitizen is 100% in this cluster, which is unique and very strong separation. This cluster has the highest churn rate at 41.62%, which is about 15 percentage points above the overall churn rate (26.54%).
Average monthly charges are also the highest (about 83.70). Contract is 54.14% month-to-month, 23.51% one-year, and 22.35% two-year. PhoneService is 100% “Yes”. InternetService is mixed (57.61% DSL, 42.39% Fiber optic).
MultipleLines is 42.77% Yes and 57.23% No.
This is the priority churn segment. The combination of being seniors and having higher monthly charges strongly suggests price sensitivity and service complexity issues.
Even with some long-term contracts present, churn remains high, indicating that contract length alone does not fully protect this group. Seniors may require clearer plan structures, stronger support, and reduced perceived hassle.
This segment should receive proactive retention interventions.

Overall conclusion
Cluster 4 is the key churn-risk segment and should be prioritised for retention.
Cluster 0 is the most stable group (two-year contract loyalists). Clusters 1–3 show churn close to the overall average and can be managed with contract conversion, value messaging, and targeted bundling strategies.
